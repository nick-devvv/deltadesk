# Few 0DTE strategies survive honest testing

No strategy for 0DTE SPY or QQQ options has top-grade evidence behind it. The academic work is almost entirely on SPX, where option buyers pay an intraday variance risk premium and sellers earn a small, fat-tailed premium that is statistically weak unless conditioned on something; nobody has published a large-sample, cost-inclusive test on SPY/QQQ 0DTE quotes. The best-documented directional signal is the one the desk is keeping: Zarattini, Aziz and Barbon's "noise area" momentum. Four independent replications reproduce it through 2024, but it has earned roughly nothing on SPY/ES since 2025 and only a statistically insignificant positive return on Nasdaq futures, and no one has tested it on options. The strongest short-premium candidates are an opening-range-conditioned credit spread (profit factor 1.44), mid-morning iron flies and condors, and VIX1D expected-move spreads. All three are vendor backtests that win 70–90% of the time and model no costs, and their $25–40 average SPX trade is about the size of a realistic round-trip spread once scaled to SPY. The discretionary ledgers (failed breakout, prior-day retest, MACD video, VWAP+MACD, stocks-in-play, earnings) have no tested analog and should be retired. Opening-range and VWAP-reclaim survive only if recoded as exact published rules. Alpaca's free history has option bars and trades back to February 2024 but no historical bid/ask, so fills must be modeled from a spread curve the desk logs itself from Schwab chains. That window of about 690 trading days is barely enough to detect a 55% win rate (617 trades needed), too short to confirm a 90%-win credit spread at profit factor 1.3 (810 needed), and supports only about ten honestly tested configurations. The $25,000 pattern-day-trader rule has been gone at Schwab since June 2026, so what still stands between the desk and automation is statistics and plumbing, not regulation.

## Buyers pay a premium that sellers collect thinly

Everyone agrees which side of same-day index options has the edge; they disagree about how big it is. In Cboe trade data tagged by trader type, **retail long 0DTE SPX positions lost 53% of their value over the full sample and 61% after daily expiries began in May 2022, while retail short positions returned 48% and 56%** ([Swedroe summary of Beckmeyer, Branger & Gayda](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/equities/retail-investors-are-in-love-with-same-day-options)). Retail was mostly long, so it lost in aggregate: more than $358,000 a day after daily expiries began ([BNN Bloomberg](https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/day-traders-lose-358-000-per-day-gambling-on-zero-day-options-1.1910634)). Researchers disagree on how much of that loss is transaction cost. Beckmeyer et al. attribute about 60% to costs ([Swedroe](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/equities/retail-investors-are-in-love-with-same-day-options)). An SEC DERA working paper on SPXW finds that customers using resting limit orders pay "realized spreads ... always less than half the quoted spread", about $0.021 versus $0.05 for a marketable order on a cheap two-tick call. It concludes that "the retail public may be losing money on these trades but they are not losing it to transactions costs" ([SEC DERA](https://www.sec.gov/files/dera-hope-reasonable-prc-2503.pdf)). Both findings hold if the difference is order type. That makes limit-versus-market execution a first-order P&L variable for the desk, on par with which signal it trades.

Systematic tests of mechanical structures point the same way and warn about size. Vilkov's SPXW study covers September 2016 to January 2026, enters at 10:00 ET and holds to the close across seven multi-leg structures. It concludes that **"unconditional 0DTE is weak after costs and tail risk,"** while conditional out-of-sample rules on selected strategies "reach a Sharpe ratio of roughly 1.0 to 1.3" ([Vilkov replication-package listing](https://tomevault.io/tome/vilkovgr/0dte-strategies)). An earlier version found that buying deep in-the-money calls and selling OTM calls and puts were profitable at the median. Most average returns were statistically insignificant, though, and the index's realized skewness explained much of the P&L ([Hull Tactical](https://hulltactical.com/2026/07/08/0dte-options-tiny-edge-huge-distribution/)). Chong's 2020–2024 volatility-arbitrage tests find large embedded premia but conclude "0DTEs are expensive but do not appear to be mispriced" once costs and estimation risk are counted ([HKUST](https://bm.hkust.edu.hk/bizinsight/2026/04/are-0dte-options-mispriced)). Almeida and Hizmeri find intraday investors pay most to cover upside moves. They also find that a strategy exploiting price-bound violations "was highly profitable through 2022" before its profits "disappeared after daily 0DTE expirations became available" ([Tinbergen Institute](https://tinbergen.nl/event/2025/11/06/13089/0dte-asset-pricing)). Two practical conclusions follow. Cheap OTM 0DTE calls are among the most overpriced things a buyer can purchase, and any edge that looked large before 2022 should be presumed gone.

The only SPY-specific measurement in the notes is a vendor study, and it agrees. It covers 193 Wednesday sessions from July 2022 to April 2026, using quote mids at 10:00 ET. **SPY closed inside the 0DTE straddle's implied one-sigma band 71.0% of the time, against 68.3% under fair pricing. The median implied move was 0.72%; the median realized move was 0.37%** ([FlashAlpha](https://flashalpha.com/articles/are-0dte-straddles-overpriced-193-spy-sessions-data-study)). The same study records the counterweight. On 2025-04-09 the implied move was 3.51% and SPY closed 8.72% above its 10:00 price. OptionMetrics finds the SPX 0DTE premium at 10:00 is positive on average with positive skew (+1.33), unlike single stocks ([OptionMetrics](https://optionmetrics.com/?p=3155)). Spread costs come on top of that. One snapshot found SPY 0DTE options near $1 quoted about 5.8% wide as a share of premium five minutes before the close, against about 1% for other expiries. An at-the-money 0DTE was quoted at $0.14/$0.15 ([Schaeffer's](https://www.schaeffersresearch.com/content/bgs/2023/09/22/0dte-options-are-higher-spreads-imminent)).

Dealer gamma is real, but its effect is small and outsiders can't see it, so it can't be traded directly. Adams, Dim, Eraker, Fontaine, Ornthanalai and Vilkov use signed Cboe Open-Close flow for 2018–2024. They find **market makers net long gamma in about 88% of 10-minute intervals**, which damps volatility and strengthens reversal. The momentum gap between the highest and lowest gamma quartiles is only −1.06, −1.78 and −2.89 bps at 30, 60 and 120 minutes. The authors say the result "does not translate into a feasible trading strategy" because outsiders cannot observe the positions ([Adams et al.](https://www.jean-sebastienfontaine.com/papers/0dte-options-volatility.pdf)). Cboe puts net market-maker 0DTE hedging at "at best 0.2% of SPX daily liquidity" ([Cboe](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/0-dt-es-decoded-positioning-trends-and-market-impact)). The desk currently buys 0.45–0.65 delta options with a 20% premium ceiling. Every such trade starts behind by the variance premium plus two half-spreads, and the signal has to clear both before being right on direction makes money. The biggest gap is that **no large-sample academic study of SPY or QQQ 0DTE returns was found**. Every number above comes from SPX, NDX or a vendor sample.

## Six candidate ledgers, none above grade B

The grades here are strict because the instrument is unforgiving. **Grade A** would require peer-reviewed or multiple independent, cost-inclusive tests on SPY/QQQ 0DTE options with positive out-of-sample results, and nothing qualifies. **Grade B** means a large sample with costs modeled and either an independent replication or a live record. The catch is that the evidence is on a different instrument (futures, shares, SPX) or shows documented post-publication decay. **Grade C** means a single vendor or author, no costs, an unstated period, or second-hand reporting. The ranking weighs grade first. It then weighs how directly the rules transfer to a SPY/QQQ desk that enters between 09:45 and 15:15 and is flat by 15:25.

The noise-area variants lead because they are the only candidates with independent, cost-inclusive replications that use honest next-bar fills. Their weakness is the instrument. Every result is on shares or futures, and the per-trade edge of +2 to +6 bps of the underlying has to survive theta and spread once expressed in options (next section). QQQ ranks above SPY because Nasdaq was stronger than S&P in every replication that tested both.

Among short-premium structures, the opening-range credit spread matters most to this desk. It is the only quantified 0DTE opening-range rule set found anywhere, and it turns the desk's discretionary ORB ledger into something testable. It bets that a breakout will not fully reverse through the opening range, and it places the short strike on the far side of the range. Its high win rate comes from that placement rather than from timing skill. The 60-minute version is clearly best (PF 1.44); shorter ranges drop to PF 1.17–1.19 with more than twice the drawdown. The results come from SPX, and dollar edges shrink with notional when moved to SPY. XSP is one-tenth of SPX and similar in size to SPY ([Cboe](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/why-trade-xsp-vs-spy-a-breakdown-of-the-benefits)), so the $15-wide SPX spread maps to roughly a $1.50-wide SPY spread. Its $39 average trade becomes about **$3.90 per SPY spread**. That has to be set against an ATM SPY 0DTE quote one cent wide ($1 per contract per leg) in the Schaeffer's example. Crossing two legs at the full spread costs $2 on entry alone, and per-contract fees do not shrink with notional. On that arithmetic, the SPY version could hand half its edge to friction even with decent limit fills. The VIX1D spread has the same problem: $25.73 per $5 SPX spread becomes about $2.57 per $0.50-wide SPY spread.

The tastytrade condor study is the only structured evidence on where to put a stop. A 2× stop cut CVaR by about 13% and a 0.5× stop cut it by more than half, but the tight stop roughly halved return on capital. About 48% of trades stopped at 0.5× would have recovered to breakeven by expiration ([StockWire X](https://stockwirex.com/?p=47372)). The structure's own arithmetic shows how thin the margin is. With a $500 credit and a $1,500 maximum loss, if every losing trade lost the maximum, the break-even win rate would be 75%, above the 72% observed. The positive average therefore depends on most losers being partial losses. The Option Alpha population study is the only large sample dominated by SPY. Its most useful number is a sobering prior: **iron condors won 70.19% of trades, yet only 49.64% of the users trading them made money** ([Option Alpha](https://optionalpha.com/blog/0dte-options-strategy-performance)). Its contrarian SMA5 credit spreads (PF above 2.0) came from an optimized curve of 37,894 trades, which is mined by construction.

Two reference rows round out the picture without being ledgers the desk can copy. Big ERN's live record is the longest cost-inclusive 0DTE record found. It sells SPX options with deltas "usually below 0.01" and stops set at roughly 10–15 times the premium ([ERN 2024](https://earlyretirementnow.com/2025/01/14/options-trading-series-part-13-year-2024-review/)), and it traded more than $6.5 billion of notional in 2025 ([ERN 2025](https://earlyretirementnow.com/2026/01/30/options-trading-series-part-14-year-2025-review)). Its 0DTE legs were also its weakest. 0DTE puts captured 32.2% of premium in 2024 and 62.2% in 2025, and 0DTE calls 61.5% and 53.1%, against 94% for the overnight 1DTE puts in 2025 ([ERN 2025](https://earlyretirementnow.com/2026/01/30/options-trading-series-part-14-year-2025-review)). Vilkov's conditional selection is the most credible academic route into short premium. The next concrete step is to pull the replication package and extract its conditioning features, then pre-register a SPY spread version.

### Win rates of 70–90% buy rare, outsized losses

Every short-premium candidate wins most days. That high win rate is built into the structure and says nothing about whether the strategy has an edge. Option Alpha's late-day condor shows the trade-off: a 0.2%-OTM, $5-wide SPX condor at 2:44 PM collected $196 against $304 of risk and reached max profit about 68% of the time, for a simplified EV of +$36 ([Option Alpha](https://optionalpha.com/learn/top-0dte-options-strategies)). If every loser were a max loss, its break-even win rate would be 61%. FlashAlpha modeled 18.3 million SPY put spreads in the shortest bucket (0–7 DTE, 0.20–0.30 delta, $5 wide). The average credit was $0.68, the realized gain only $0.042, and the win rate 83.1%. The author warns that "a $0.10–0.20 round-trip cost erases most of the edge" ([FlashAlpha](https://flashalpha.com/articles/spy-put-credit-spread-matrix-8-year-backtest-theoretical-vs-realized)). tastylive found that the same short strategy collected five to six times more premium at 0DTE than at 45 DTE, while the same market move produced roughly 30 times more risk ([tastylive](https://www.tastylive.com/news-insights/0dte-options-risk-of-naked-short-premium)).

The losses cluster on a handful of days, and the desk's Alpaca window contains most of them. On **2025-04-09**, SPY closed 8.72% above its 10:00 price against a 3.51% implied move ([FlashAlpha](https://flashalpha.com/articles/are-0dte-straddles-overpriced-193-spy-sessions-data-study)). The S&P rose 10.39% from the open, ERN's 0DTE stops triggered, and his April premium was "largely lost" ([ERN 2025](https://earlyretirementnow.com/2026/01/30/options-trading-series-part-14-year-2025-review)). On the **2024-12-18 FOMC** day, ERN was stopped out, re-sold further-OTM puts and was stopped again, a "two-for-one loss" ([ERN 2024](https://earlyretirementnow.com/2025/01/14/options-trading-series-part-13-year-2024-review/)). A seller of one-day NDX straddles won 10 of 12 FOMC meetings for +450 points net. The same 2024-12-18 straddle, priced at 187.40 and settling at 790.69, lost about 603 points by itself ([Nasdaq/Rhoads](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/fomc-volatility-premium-evidence-1-day-nasdaq-100-straddles)). On **2024-08-05**, pre-market VIX hit 65 and 0DTE implied vol about 80% ([ORATS](https://orats.com/blog/0dte-and-the-august-fifth-correction)). ERN was down about four to five months of options income at midday before recovering by the close. Stops on these days fill worse than modeled. One breakeven-condor example lost −$926 against a planned −$154 when price gapped ([OptionsTradingIQ](https://optionstradingiq.com/break-even-iron-condor-strategy/)). The April 2025 shock was a violent rally, so tests must cover both tails. Cboe's Henry Schwartz skips Fed days, the day before major data, and extreme-uncertainty days such as Apr 9 and Aug 4, 2025 ([Cboe](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/henry-schwartzs-zero-day-spx-iron-condor-strategy-a-deep-dive)). An event-calendar filter of that kind is the cheapest tail control available.

Long premium has the mirror-image profile: many small losses and rare large wins. On Nasdaq micros, the noise-area strategy won on only 24% of days, and a few trend days drove the profit ([VigorTas](https://github.com/VigorTas/beat-the-market-replication)). A simulation run for this report puts numbers on that. At a 38% win rate over 690 trades, the median longest losing streak is 12 trades, and one run in ten has a streak of 16 or more. A community test buying ITM 0DTE SPY calls on a 9/21 EMA cross reported "a long run of losses between July 2023 to Jan 2025" ([Reddit r/algotrading snapshot](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1t59fsz/snapshots/2026-05-08T19%3A59%3A29.3336Z)). The two books lose on opposite days. The authors claim the noise-area strategy's Sharpe reached 3.50 when VIX was above 40 ([Quantified Strategies](https://quantifiedstrategies.substack.com/p/systematic-intraday-trend-following)), and those trend-heavy days are exactly when short condors lose. The daily P&L correlation between a momentum ledger and a condor ledger is worth measuring before sizing either.

## The noise band replicated, then faded after publication

The published rules are simple enough to code exactly. The original study used 1-minute SPY data from May 2007 to April 2024. It enters only at hour and half-hour marks on a band break, stops at the opposite band or the max/min of band and VWAP, and is flat at the close. Costs were modeled at $0.0035 per share commission plus $0.001 per share slippage ([CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/momentum-investing/complex-intraday-time-series-momentum-strategy-applied-to-spy/)). The desk's `noiseband.py` follows the same 14-session, per-time-of-day band and the max(band, VWAP) stop. The authors claim **1,985% total return, 19.6% annualized, Sharpe 1.33 against 0.45 for buy-and-hold, and Sharpe 3.50 when VIX exceeds 40**. They say it "has continued to deliver strong performance in out-of-sample testing" but give no figures ([Quantified Strategies](https://quantifiedstrategies.substack.com/p/systematic-intraday-trend-following)). That summary also says slippage was not modeled, which conflicts with CXO's account, and adds "We have not conducted our own backtests" ([Quantified Strategies](https://quantifiedstrategies.substack.com/p/systematic-intraday-trend-following)). The paper took fourth place in the Quantpedia Awards 2025, which is recognition rather than performance evidence ([FSI St. Gallen](https://fsi.unisg.ch/de/newsdetail/news/quantpedia-awards-2025-fsis-barbon-and-team-secure-4th-place-with-intraday-momentum-strategy/)).

Independent replications now agree on the shape of the result. Brusco froze the paper's parameters ex ante (14-day lookback, 30-minute checks, 2% vol target, 4× cap) and ran them on SPY from 2020 to 2026. He got **Sharpe 1.11, alpha of 16.7% a year (t = 2.85), +2.6 bps per trade, a 41% win rate and a 1.69 payoff ratio**. Each year from 2020 to 2024 had a Sharpe between 1.4 and 2.0, and 2022 returned +25.8% while SPY fell 19.5%. But two later years were negative, and the recent Sharpe is about zero on both SPY and ES. His verdict is that the strategy is "not allocable today" but "not dismissible as dead" ([Brusco](https://github.com/giovannibrusco/zarattini-2024-momentum-spy)). VigorTas used one micro contract with next-bar-open fills and a tick of slippage on every fill. **Nasdaq futures were significant over the full period (t = 2.74) and positive after publication ($13.90 a day, t = 1.37), but S&P futures lost money both overall (t = −0.20) and after publication (t = −1.21)** ([VigorTas](https://github.com/VigorTas/beat-the-market-replication)). Quantitativo found ES at paper settings returned 8.1% CAGR against a 12.4% benchmark (Sharpe 0.91, max drawdown 24%), while NQ reached Sharpe 1.67. The author notes the strategy was roughly flat from 2010 to 2017, that half a tick of slippage per trade is "already optimistic," and that the model failed on most single stocks ([Quantitativo](https://www.quantitativo.com/p/intraday-momentum-for-es-and-nq)). Concretum's own February 2026 note reports an intraday trend baseline with Sharpe 0.87 over 2007–2026 ([Concretum](https://concretumgroup.com/quantip-improving-performance-with-fast-alphas-a-tactical-overlay-for-intraday-trend-trading/)). Against 1.33 for 2007–2024, that is consistent with post-2024 dilution, though the note does not confirm it is the identical strategy.

Tuning makes things worse, so the parameters should stay frozen. Maróy's 2025 optimization paper claims "Sharpe ratios over 3.0 and annualized returns of over 50%" using VWAP and ladder exits, with no sample, costs or out-of-sample test described ([Bear Bull Traders forum](https://forums.bearbulltraders.com/topic/4002-improvements-to-intraday-momentum-strategies-using-parameter-optimization-and-different-exit-strategies/)). Brusco reran Maróy's 27-variant grid. The best in-sample variant was the paper's original configuration. **Re-selecting the best variant each quarter on its trailing 252-day Sharpe produced Sharpe 0.57 against 0.92 for the frozen settings.** It made 14 switches in 19 quarters and never once picked the full-sample winner ([Brusco](https://github.com/giovannibrusco/zarattini-2024-momentum-spy)). Quantitativo's improvement from a 90-day lookback was found after seeing results and counts as in-sample.

No one has run the noise band on options, and the options arithmetic is tight. The payoff shape fits long options well: a win rate of 36–41% with winners 1.7–2.25 times the size of losers, where convexity can enlarge trend-day winners. But +2.6 bps of SPY at a $600 price is about $0.16 per share, or roughly $0.10 of option P&L at 0.65 delta, before theta and spread (illustrative arithmetic from [Brusco](https://github.com/giovannibrusco/zarattini-2024-momentum-spy)'s per-trade edge). That is small next to a median 10:00 implied move of 0.72% ([FlashAlpha](https://flashalpha.com/articles/are-0dte-straddles-overpriced-193-spy-sessions-data-study)). Market structure also leans against it: dealers are usually long gamma ([Adams et al.](https://www.jean-sebastienfontaine.com/papers/0dte-options-volatility.pdf)) and realized moves run below implied. QQQ, with a historical edge of +6 bps per trade, is the better underlying. In-the-money strikes carry less extrinsic value and so pay less of the variance premium. Vilkov's finding that deep ITM calls were profitable at the median points the same way ([Hull Tactical](https://hulltactical.com/2026/07/08/0dte-options-tiny-edge-huge-distribution/)).

The desk's own noise ledger (61 trades, profit factor 1.67) cannot yet be told apart from the near-zero recent Sharpe the replications report. At 61 trades, the 95% confidence interval on a win rate is ±12.5 percentage points. Three changes would make it informative without breaking the rule that the original keeps running. First, split SPY and QQQ into separate ledgers, because the replications show they diverge. Second, recognize that the 20% premium ceiling checked on every tick is a deviation from the published rules, which exit only on the band/VWAP stop at half-hour checks. Theta and noise alone can trigger it. Log how often it fires before the band stop would have, and run a versioned twin without it. Third, treat a volatility-regime filter (VIX above 40, or high VIX1D) as a pre-registered hypothesis, not a proven filter: the 3.50 Sharpe figure is in-sample and such days are rare. Concretum's "fast alphas" overlay waits for short counter-moves before entering a trend signal. It lifted the trend baseline from Sharpe 0.87 to 0.99 ([Concretum](https://concretumgroup.com/quantip-improving-performance-with-fast-alphas-a-tactical-overlay-for-intraday-trend-trading/)), which makes it a plausible way to cut option entry cost, but its exact rules sit in the PDF.

The VWAP case deserves one more note. The paper's rule flips long and short on the side of VWAP, so on choppy days it flips repeatedly. In options, each flip pays a full bid-ask round trip and buys fresh extrinsic value, so a share-based backtest is a poor proxy. Concretum's own February 2026 work makes the point from the other direction. A streak-based SPY mean-reversion signal showed **gross Sharpe above 2 and about 31.9% gross CAGR but was unprofitable after costs on shares** ([Concretum](https://concretumgroup.com/quantip-improving-performance-with-fast-alphas-a-tactical-overlay-for-intraday-trend-trading/)). Long 0DTE options only raise the cost bar, so reversal-style ledgers such as failed breakouts and level retests start at a disadvantage.

The broader record shows well-known intraday index anomalies decaying in 2016–2025, which argues for validating any new signal on 2022–2026 data alone. **Last-half-hour market momentum is flat in the 0DTE era.** Across 1,085 SPX sessions from April 2022 to August 2026, the slope is +0.006 (t = 0.6), and its sign alternates by year. It reappears only on the roughly 15% of days when a tape-signed dealer book is short gamma (t = 3.1), and **a book built from public open interest shows no effect** ([FirmTape](https://dev.to/firmtape/intraday-momentum-is-dead-in-the-0dte-era-we-measured-it-on-1085-spx-sessions-43g0)). A gamma filter built from Schwab open interest should therefore be expected to fail. The desk's own 28-session observation that the first-period/last-period relationship ran in reverse is consistent with this null result. The pre-FOMC drift fell from about 49 bps (1994–2011) to an insignificant 9 bps (2016–2019) ([Kurov, Wolfe & Gilbert](https://www.skidmore.edu/economics/documents/KurovWolfeGilbert-TheDisappearingPre-FOMC-Announce-Drift-200914.pdf)). The 2–3 a.m. ET overnight drift in ES averaged close to zero over 2021–2025 ([Liberty Street Economics](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2026/07/the-disappearing-overnight-drift/)). In the cross-section of stocks, end-of-day returns reverse rather than continue ([Baltussen, Da & Soebhag](https://academicweb.nd.edu/~zda/EOD.pdf)).

Community tests help mainly as a list of failure modes; none establishes an edge. Reddit threads with real backtests were small or covered a single regime. One protected-spread system reported a 53% win rate on January–August 2023 data with "near-mid price fills" ([Reddit snapshot](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1rg6nt0/snapshots/2026-02-27T22%3A12%3A05.0165Z)). Commenters on the EMA-cross test estimated real P&L at "30-50 percent" below the backtest ([Reddit snapshot](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1t59fsz/snapshots/2026-05-08T19%3A59%3A29.3336Z)). The YouTube material found was vendor marketing. An Option Alpha video comparing 10:30 and 14:30 condor entries cites "a $15,000 difference in P/L" without saying which entry won ([Option Alpha](https://optionalpha.com/videos/morning-vs-afternoon-0dte-backtested-options-strategies)). Quantified Strategies calls ORB strategies "almost completely obsolete," but offers no data on that page ([Quantified Strategies](https://quantifiedstrategies.substack.com/p/opening-range-breakout-trading-strategies)). The most relevant unread source is Kam's "Empirical Study of 3,909 Option Alpha Backtests" ([SSRN 7055179](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7055179)). If it is accessible, it is the closest thing to a large cross-strategy test of retail 0DTE rules.

## Alpaca's free history records prints, not quotes

What Alpaca's free account provides is specific. The docs state, **"Currently we only offer historical option data since February 2024."** ([Alpaca docs](https://docs.alpaca.markets/docs/historical-option-data)). The option endpoints are bars, trades, latest trade, latest quote and snapshots. **There is no historical quotes endpoint, only "latest"** ([Alpaca MCP server OpenAPI spec](https://github.com/alpacahq/alpaca-mcp-server)). Alpaca runs two option feeds. The free "Indicative Pricing Feed is a free derivative of the original OPRA feed": its quotes are derived rather than real OPRA quotes, and "the trades are also derivatives and they're delayed by 15 minutes," while the "OPRA feed is only available to subscribed users" ([Alpaca docs](https://docs.alpaca.markets/docs/historical-option-data)). The default Basic plan (paper and live) carries the indicative feed, a "Latest 15 minutes" historical restriction and 200 calls a minute. Algo Trader Plus carries OPRA, "No restriction" on history and 10,000 calls a minute. The same page lists $99 a month for the equities version and shows no separate options price ([Alpaca market data plans](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/about-market-data-api.md)). Two things remain unverified. One is whether historical bars on the Basic plan come from OPRA or the indicative feed. The other is the meaning of "Latest 15 minutes," which most likely means queries must end at least 15 minutes before the present. The desk should settle both by comparing a sample of Alpaca bars with Schwab prints logged the same day. The data also has holes: a user reported OPRA trade history missing the whole morning session on 2024-02-26 and 2024-03-15 across several contracts ([Alpaca forum](https://forum.alpaca.markets/t/missing-morning-session-in-historical-option-trades-2024-02-26-and-2024-03-15-opra/19028)). Expired contracts stay queryable, so the free path yields 1-minute bars for SPY and QQQ 0DTE strikes over roughly 690 trading days. That window conveniently includes August 5, 2024, December 18, 2024 and April 2025.

Without historical bid/ask, fills have to be modeled, and the model should be built conservatively. Bars are trade prints. A fill should be priced from the first bar that closes after the signal, never the signal bar itself, at close plus or minus k half-spreads. Price longs above the print and shorts below it. The half-spread should come from an empirical curve, not a constant. Fit it on Schwab chain snapshots the desk logs every minute from now on, as a function of delta, minutes to the close, option price level and VIX1D. Then sweep k from 0.5 to 1.0: **a strategy whose edge disappears at k = 1 is not robust**. Numerix recommends buying at the ask and selling at the bid as the backtest default ([Numerix](https://www.numerix.com/sites/default/files/file/2026-01/Numerix_White_Paper_0DTE_Trading_Strategies.pdf)). Alpaca's own 0DTE spread notebook exits shorts at the ask and longs at the bid on real Databento quotes ([Alpaca Learn](https://alpaca.markets/learn/backtesting-zero-dte-bull-put-spread-options-strategy-with-python)). Multi-leg structures should pay the half-spread on every leg unless the desk's paper fills prove that package fills do better. Stops should fill at the next bar's adverse price plus a gap haircut, never at the trigger, because the ERN and breakeven-condor cases show stops filling far through their levels. Far-OTM strikes with missing bars should be treated as unfillable, not interpolated. As a starting prior until the desk's own curve exists, SPY 0DTE spreads run near 6% of premium for $1 options late in the day ([Schaeffer's](https://www.schaeffersresearch.com/content/bgs/2023/09/22/0dte-options-are-higher-spreads-imminent)). SPX retail effective spreads ran 6.1% for calls and 4.9% for puts ([Swedroe](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/equities/retail-investors-are-in-love-with-same-day-options)).

## Two point seven years buys about ten honest experiments

Sample size is where most 0DTE claims fall apart, and the numbers are unforgiving for a one-trade-a-day strategy. The figures below were worked from standard one-proportion and mean tests and checked again for this report. They frame what the Alpaca window can and cannot prove. For a strategy that actually wins 55% of trades against a 50% null, **an observed 55% becomes significant at 271 trades one-sided or 385 two-sided, and reliably detecting a true 55% with 80% power takes 617 trades one-sided or 783 two-sided.** The confidence interval around a win rate shrinks slowly:

| Trades | 95% CI half-width on a win rate near 50% |
|---|---|
| 40 | ±15.5 pp |
| 50 | ±13.9 pp |
| 61 (current noise ledger) | ±12.5 pp |
| 100 | ±9.8 pp |
| 200 | ±6.9 pp |
| 500 | ±4.4 pp |
| 1,000 | ±3.1 pp |

Profit factor tells a sharper story, because testing PF > 1 is the same as testing mean trade P&L > 0, and the answer depends on payoff shape. For two-outcome trades with a 1-unit loss and the average win set to give PF = 1.3:

| Profile | Win rate | Avg win (loss = 1) | Per-trade μ/σ | n, one-sided 95%, observed at boundary | n, one-sided 95%, 80% power | n, two-sided 95%, 80% power |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Long premium / lottery | 30% | 3.03 | 0.114 | 210 | 479 | 609 |
| Symmetric | 50% | 1.30 | 0.130 | 160 | 364 | 462 |
| Credit spread | 80% | 0.325 | 0.113 | 212 | 483 | 613 |
| High-win-rate credit | 90% | 0.144 | 0.087 | 355 | **810** | 1,029 |

Real short-premium P&L has fat left tails, so its true σ is larger and its required sample larger still. A handy rule of thumb is n ≈ (2.5 / per-trade Sharpe)². The Alpaca window offers at most about 690 trades per one-a-day strategy per underlying. **It can falsify a high-win-rate credit strategy but cannot confirm one**, and it contains only a handful of the shock days that decide that strategy's fate. Minimum track record length on daily returns makes the same point: an annualized Sharpe of 1.0 needs 684 days (2.7 years) with normal returns and 775 days (3.1 years) with short-vol-like skew of −2 and kurtosis of 10. A Sharpe of 2.0 needs 173 and about 220 days. The formulas behind these tables come from Bailey and López de Prado's Probabilistic and Deflated Sharpe work ([SSRN 2460551](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551)) and should be checked against the PDFs.

Multiple testing raises the bar further, because the best of many variants looks good by luck alone. Suppose the variants' daily Sharpe ratios have a spread of 0.05, about 0.8 annualized. Then the expected best annualized Sharpe from pure noise is **1.25 after 10 trials, 1.81 after 50, 2.01 after 100 and 2.58 after 1,000.** A "Sharpe 2" winner picked from about 100 parameter combinations is consistent with zero skill. Turned around as a minimum backtest length (the years of data needed before zero-skill trials stop producing an in-sample Sharpe of 1), the requirement is about 2.5 years for 10 trials, 5.0 for 45, 6.4 for 100 and 10.6 for 1,000. With about 2.7 years of real option history, **the desk can honestly afford on the order of ten independent configurations**. Harvey, Liu and Zhu argue a new finding "needs to clear a much higher hurdle, with a t-ratio greater than 3.0" and that "most claimed research findings in financial economics are likely false" ([NBER w20592](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20592)). The Probability of Backtest Overfitting framework estimates how often the in-sample winner underperforms out of sample, using combinatorially symmetric cross-validation, and argues that standard hold-outs are unreliable for backtests ([SSRN 2326253](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2326253)). White's Reality Check ([Econometrica](https://doi.org/10.1111/1468-0262.00152)) and Hansen's Superior Predictive Ability test ([JBES](https://doi.org/10.1198/073500105000000063)) test whether the best of M strategies beats a benchmark after accounting for all M tried. Hansen's version is less distorted by adding poor strategies to the family.

The protocol that follows from this is concrete and mostly free. Every variant tried gets logged, with its count and the spread of its Sharpe ratios, so a deflated Sharpe can be computed; the desk's `strategy_registry.py` attempt log and spec hashing already do the bookkeeping. Signals get tested on the underlying first over long history, then as options over the Feb-2024-onward window. Because of the decay record, the underlying test should also be reported for 2022–2026 alone. Option tests run as an anchored walk-forward: train from February 2024 through month m, test on month m+1, roll monthly, giving about 20 out-of-sample months. Combinatorial purged cross-validation on daily P&L with a one-day embargo is a cheap cross-check, since one 0DTE trade is roughly one day. Confidence intervals on profit factor, win rate and drawdown come from a stationary block bootstrap on daily P&L ([Politis & Romano](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1994.10476870)) with mean blocks of 5–10 days, which keeps volatility clustering intact. The bar for calling something discovered is t > 3. Brusco's walk-forward result, where adaptive reselection lost to frozen parameters, is direct evidence that the ten-configuration budget should go to structurally different ideas rather than parameter grids. The desk's current promotion rule of 40 forward paper trades carries a ±15.5-point uncertainty on win rate. That is enough to catch a broken fill model and far too few to confirm an edge. Forward paper trading is best framed as a test of whether modeled fills match real ones, with promotion resting on the walk-forward and deflated statistics.

## Automation needs read-only AI and hard-coded limits

The regulatory barrier that shaped small-account day trading is gone. **The SEC approved FINRA's replacement for the pattern-day-trader rule on April 14, 2026, effective June 4, 2026, with brokers given until October 20, 2027 to implement.** The $25,000 minimum and the PDT designation are eliminated, day trades are no longer counted, and "eligible margin accounts of more than $2,000 will gain access to intraday margin buying power." **Schwab stopped counting day trades on June 8, 2026** and monitors intraday buying power in real time ([Schwab](https://advisorservices.schwab.com/story/sec-approves-scrapping-25000-day-trader-minimum); [Bloomberg Government](https://news.bgov.com/securities-law/sec-approves-plan-removing-day-trading-limit-for-investors-1); [E*TRADE](https://us.etrade.com/knowledge/library/margin/pattern-day-trading-rule-change)). Alpaca's implementation date was not checked and can fall anywhere before the October 2027 deadline. Defined-risk spreads need Schwab option Level 2, which adds "Spreads" above Level 1's long options, according to an older StreetSmart Pro document. Iron condors are not named there and likely fall under Level 2 as two spreads ([Schwab](https://help.streetsmart.schwab.com/pro/4.36/Content/Option_Approval_Levels.htm)).

The constraints that remain are operational. A third-party guide to the Schwab Trader API reports several limits. Order writes are throttled to between 0 and 120 a minute per account, set per application at registration. Access tokens last 30 minutes, and refresh tokens are single-use with "a fresh seven days." There is no sandbox ([dp_exchange_schwab guide](https://repo.hex.pm/preview/dp_exchange_schwab/0.1.5/usage-rules.md)). These need confirming in Schwab's own developer portal. An unattended 0DTE bot built on them needs three things. It needs an order-rate governor. It needs a weekly re-authentication alert, because a lapsed token means no exits. And it needs a fail-safe for open positions if the API fails near the close. Short-premium ledgers on SPY and QQQ carry a risk SPX does not. Both are American-style and physically settled, so short legs face early assignment and after-the-close "contra exercise." XSP is European-style and cash-settled at one-tenth of SPX, with possible Section 1256 tax treatment ([Cboe](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/why-trade-xsp-vs-spy-a-breakdown-of-the-benefits)). The desk's 15:25 flat rule neutralizes most of the assignment risk. It also gives up the steepest decay, which accelerates after 15:30 ([Option Alpha](https://optionalpha.com/blog/0dte-options-time-decay)). Every hold-to-close rule in Table 2 therefore becomes a tested variant when run on this desk.

On the Claude side, the useful tools are third-party MCP servers, not Anthropic's finance plugins. Anthropic's financial-services marketplace targets banking, equity research, private equity and fund administration. Its README says the agents "do not make investment recommendations, execute transactions," and its data connectors are paid enterprise sources ([anthropics/financial-services-plugins](https://github.com/anthropics/financial-services-plugins)). Alpaca's official MCP server v2 is a ground-up rewrite in which "None of the V1 tools exist in V2." It defaults to paper trading. It exposes option bars, trades, latest quotes, snapshots, chains and single- or multi-leg option orders, and an `ALPACA_TOOLSETS` setting can strip the `trading` toolset server-side ([alpacahq/alpaca-mcp-server](https://github.com/alpacahq/alpaca-mcp-server)). jkoelker/schwab-mcp offers a "Basic Read-Only Mode (Safest)." Orders are previewed and then placed by ID "so the LLM can't hallucinate a different order than what was reviewed," with optional Discord approval; a `--jesus-take-the-wheel` flag that bypasses approval is marked "DANGER" ([jkoelker/schwab-mcp](https://github.com/jkoelker/schwab-mcp)). The TypeScript sudowealth server marks its order tools experimental and was last updated in June 2025 ([sudowealth/schwab-mcp](https://github.com/sudowealth/schwab-mcp)). For backtesting, optopsy is a Python options library with 38 strategies, per-leg delta targeting and slippage models. It is AGPL-licensed and needs bid/ask chain data, and its own disclaimer says it does not model liquidity, slippage or assignment risk ([goldspanlabs/optopsy](https://github.com/goldspanlabs/optopsy)). optopsy-mcp wraps a Rust rewrite as an MCP server with sandboxed strategy scripts and grid or Bayesian sweeps ([goldspanlabs/optopsy-mcp](https://github.com/goldspanlabs/optopsy-mcp)). Every sweep inflates the trial count that the deflated Sharpe must charge for. QuantConnect LEAN is the one engine with community-reported minute-level 0DTE support and a ThetaData adapter ([Lean.DataSource.ThetaData](https://github.com/QuantConnect/Lean.DataSource.ThetaData)).

The safest configuration keeps Claude on the research side of the order path. That means Claude Code in the repository, Schwab MCP in read-only mode for live chains, and Alpaca MCP restricted to `options-data`, `assets` and `stock-data` for historical bars. It also means optopsy-mcp for quick screens and a project skill that refuses to call a strategy proven until its logged trial count, deflated Sharpe, overfitting probability and out-of-sample period pass. Any server that can place orders can be steered by injected text in the data it reads, whether news, web pages, README files or even chain fields. Execution should therefore stay in deterministic desk code that calls the broker directly, with position, loss and order-rate limits hard-coded rather than prompted. Server versions should be pinned, since Alpaca's v2 rewrite shows tool schemas can change wholesale. The desk's existing rules already point this way: paper-only `broker.py`, refused Schwab `/trader` endpoints, and a separate switch for any real-money path.

## Conclusion

The desk's binding constraint is cost measurement, not signal discovery. Every surviving candidate has a per-trade edge (+2.6 to +6 bps on the underlying for momentum, $25–40 per SPX spread for short premium) about the size of the round-trip spread once scaled to SPY. That makes a self-built spread curve, from minute-by-minute Schwab logging plus Databento's free credit, worth more than any new indicator, and it should start before any new ledger is coded. The evidence also suggests running two small books with opposite risk shapes and judging them on combined daily P&L. One is a frozen noise-area momentum book, split SPY and QQQ, with low win rates and long droughts. The other is a pre-registered, event-filtered, defined-risk short-premium book, led by the 60-minute ORB credit spread, which wins often and fails on trend days. The days that hurt one are the days that pay the other, and that hedge is untested and measurable.

Honest statistics change what "validated" can mean on this desk. With about 690 days of real option prints, the desk can reject bad ideas quickly but cannot confirm a high-win-rate credit strategy, and it has room for roughly ten serious experiments, not hundreds. Parameter grids spend that budget fastest and, on the noise-area evidence, buy nothing. The PDT repeal removes the old reason a student could not run this desk intraday. What still stands in the way is a defensible sample, a measured cost model and an execution path that keeps AI out of order placement.
